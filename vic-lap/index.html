How this lap was made

The racing line is a minimum-curvature path that keeps the car's centre at least 1.3 m inside the white lines. The car is a point-mass model of a current F1 car: 800 kg, 750 kW, drag area 1.4 m², downforce area 4.0 m², tyre grip 1.6 with downforce. The speed at every metre is the fastest the car can corner, accelerate or brake given that grip.

Run-off (v0.3) is sized by sending a car off the circuit from every point of every turn: 10% over the simulated speed, or 20 m late on the brakes at braking corners, straight on and yawing up to 10° outwards, braking at about 0.9 of normal grip. The area those paths sweep becomes run-off: asphalt for the first 35 m, gravel beyond, with energy-absorbing barriers at its edge.

It has no gears, tyre wear, fuel, DRS or driver error. Real qualifying laps on tracks with similar numbers land within a few seconds of this kind of estimate, so treat the lap time as a ballpark.
